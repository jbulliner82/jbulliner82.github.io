Learning-design case study: Leadership Under Pressure

Author/portfolio: Joseph Bulliner · October 2026

Type: self-directed scenario microlearning, prototype, not client work

Audience hypothesis: frontline leads and new managers in high-accountability operations

Delivery: self-paced HTML/JavaScript, no account or LMS required

Estimated seat time: 8–15 minutes depending on reflection and retries

Business problem (illustrative, not a validated client need)

Operational miscommunication creates avoidable rework when time-sensitive tasks pass between people with unclear information, escalation criteria, or ownership.

Performance outcomes

At the end of the learning experience, learners should be able to:

  • Confirm relevant facts, prior actions, ownership, and timing before accepting an ambiguous handoff.
  • Escalate a risk using objective evidence, impact, deadline, proposed mitigation, and a decision request.
  • Document a workaround, follow-up owner, verification date, and recurrence-prevention action.

Design model

ADDIE-inspired design with performance analysis, scenario practice, immediate feedback, and an evaluation proposal. There is no empirical learner-needs assessment, SME sign-off, or field evaluation yet.

Alignment matrix

ObjectiveScenarioTarget behaviorEvidence of learningTypical misconception
1Incomplete handoffClarify facts, actions, owner, deadlinePicks structured handoff optionMoving fast means not asking questions
2Emerging riskFacts + impact + deadline + mitigation + ownerPicks concise structured escalationUrgent vague messages are sufficient
3Closing loopCapture workaround, owner, follow-upPicks documented closure optionTemporary success is a permanent resolution

Interaction and feedback

  • Three short fictional situations with three selectable responses each.
  • Single attempt per item, immediate reasoned feedback, forward progression and completion report.
  • Results are kept in browser memory, not transmitted or stored by the website.
  • Incorrect answers are treated as practice opportunities; a retake is possible.

Accessibility / usability provisions

Semantic labels, keyboard-operable native buttons, visible focus states, responsive layout, aria-live for feedback, strong contrast, no autoplay or time limits. Formal WCAG audit and screen-reader user testing are still needed.

Evaluation strategy (proposed, not achieved)

  • Level 1: learner-reported relevance (1–5) and ease of use after a pilot.
  • Level 2: pre/post scenario responses, with non-identical equivalent scenarios.
  • Level 3: manager observation of handoff completeness and escalation quality at 30 days.
  • Level 4: compare rates of rework or missed escalations after a controlled rollout, adjusted for volume and operational changes.
  • Pilot target: 5–8 learners and 1–2 operations SMEs; revise confusing distractors and measure retake patterns.

Required validation before calling this effective training

A needs analysis, SME verification, a content review, accessibility testing, a pilot group, and actual follow-up behavioral data. No course-completion count, behavior change, or performance improvement is claimed.
